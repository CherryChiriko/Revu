The system

Mastery isn't a queue status. It's earned.

Most flashcard apps confuse "not due yet" with "known." Revu tracks depth separately from scheduling — a card can be mastered and still show up for review, because staying sharp is the whole point.

Stage 01

New

First exposure. Revu introduces the character, word, or fact and gauges your baseline recall.

Stage 02

Familiar

You're recognizing it consistently, but the interval is still short enough that it could slip.

Stage 03

Solid

Long intervals, high recall. This is where most of your deck should live day to day.

Stage 04

Mastered

Deep retention — and Revu keeps it in rotation anyway, at a distance, so it never quietly fades.

Two ways to study

Built for words and for characters.

Not every language needs the same card. Revu ships two dedicated study modes, so a French vocab deck and a Chinese character deck each get the interface they actually need.

Mode A

Standard flip cards

The classic front-and-back review, tuned for vocabulary, grammar patterns, or anything that fits on two sides of a card. Fast, keyboard-driven, no friction between you and the next rep.

Under the hood

The details that make review actually work.

None of this is flashy for its own sake — every feature exists because a plain flip card wasn't enough for real language retention.

⟲

Import in minutes

Bring an existing deck in from a spreadsheet, or clone and remix one of yours — swap fields, convert Chinese decks to flip cards, or build a remedial deck from your mistakes.

▦

Real activity data

A heatmap of your study history, streaks that survive a missed day if you're still ahead, and per-deck stats that show exactly where your time is going.

✎

Edit without leaving flow

Fix a typo, adjust a reading, or add a note mid-session — card details are always one tap away, without breaking your review streak.

◐

Themes that respect focus

A dark and light theme, both tuned in the same purple-to-blue palette, so late-night review sessions don't fight your eyes.

✦

XP that means something

Earn more for graduating a card to the next mastery stage than for a routine review — progress reflects growth, not just button-mashing.

⌘

Built for daily use

Configurable daily limits for new cards and reviews mean your queue stays sane, whether you have five minutes or fifty.
